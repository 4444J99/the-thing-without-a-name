The 2017 piece was a cubist study made by feeling. I cut where the edges wanted to go, but the fragments were never only flat pieces: each raw photograph was a view into the same room from the same camera position. Danse begins there, with the conviction that a photograph records both an instant and the place from which it was seen.

The tracked corpus is deliberately specific. It contains 161 registered raw photographs from 20 June 2017 and one unregistered archival composite made afterward. The current reconstruction draws from 76 of those photographs and the composite itself. That self-reference is kept visible rather than disguised, while new generated cuts use only the registered camera sources. Every delivered visual fragment must remain accountable to a declared record.

The ScreenDance capture asks the engine to listen before it changes. Two Delibes waltzes provide a native musical clock. A whole photograph is given time to read; the archival composite assembles and holds; the camera leaves the original viewpoint slowly; poses recur as authored motifs; and stillness lasts for a complete phrase. Development replaces flashing. Dissolves occupy musical time, and a climax grows from variation rather than erasing the field with a new seed.

The title comes from Stephen King's description in Danse Macabre of a figure assembled from other bodies. The monsters pictured on the apartment wall were not added as symbols later; they were already part of the room. The film ends in black with the identity of one fixed passage. That capture is finite, but the shared engine from which it is taken remains larger than the recording.
