The 2017 piece was a cubist study made entirely by feeling — I cut where the edges wanted to go and could not have told you why. What I could never do by hand was the thing the pictures were obviously asking for: the fragments were not flat. Each one was a window onto the same room from the same eye, and a window can be moved.

So the rule of this film is that nothing is invented. Every fragment is a real photograph of a real afternoon, addressed through the actual lens position that took it. The empty room was never photographed; it is recovered from the hundred and sixty-two pictures that have a person standing in front of it. The sound is not scored to the image, it is derived from it — the depth of each hanging plane sets the pitch, so the chord you hear is literally the arrangement you see, and every sample in it was recorded in that apartment a week before the shoot.

The title is Stephen King's, from inside Danse Macabre — his name for the Frankenstein figure, the thing made of parts of other bodies. The monsters on that wall were not set dressing. They were the reason the room looked like that.

What I want from a viewer is the moment the reproduction stops being a reproduction: the composite holds exactly long enough to be recognised, and then the room opens behind it and you understand you were looking through a keyhole. It is a portrait of someone I was with then, made out of the only thing left of it, by a process that will never make it the same way twice.
